SOUND SEARCH - search audio by how it sounds, not by words
==========================================================

Unzip the folder anywhere (not inside the zip preview). Then pick one:

1) QUICK - no setup, works offline
   Open  web\index.html  (double-click; Chrome or Edge).
   Works: both maps, every ready-made search, playing every clip, "-> similar", charts, light/dark.
   Not here: typing your own search.

2) FULL - adds typing your own search
   Windows: double-click  start.bat
   Mac:     double-click  start.command  (if blocked: right-click > Open)
   The first time it sets itself up (about 1 GB download, 5-10 minutes, needs internet once).
   After that it opens the page in your browser within seconds. Keep the black window open while presenting.
   Wait for "Models ready" before the first typed search.

   Sharing on your network instead of presenting from your laptop:
   run  start.bat --lan  and give people the "Others on this network" link it prints.


A 3-MINUTE DEMO
---------------
Real audio map (opens first):
  1. Click "News anchor reading headlines": news podcasts light up, the top one plays. No transcript was used.
  2. Click "An angry voice": typing the mood finds nothing (0 of 10).
     Then click "Angry clip -> similar": it plays an angry clip and finds mostly angry voices.
     The model hears the mood but can't name it.
  3. Click "Classical piano" or "Jazz": music by genre, scored right/wrong.
  4. Type your own: "someone nervous", "two people arguing", "late-night radio host". Leave the model on Auto.
Sound effects tab:
  5. Click "Owl hooting" or "Coughing": 9-10 of 10 right, scored against human labels.
Anywhere: click any dot on the map to hear it. Reset (top right) clears everything. Esc does the same.

Which model? Leave it on Auto. Gemma hears how people talk; CLAP knows sound effects and music.

Note: the audio comes from podcasts and research datasets with their own licences.
Internal demo use only: please don't post the clips publicly.
