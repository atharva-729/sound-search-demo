What's in the collection

How it works

  • Every clip is turned into a list of numbers (an embedding) by an audio–text model. Clips that sound alike get similar numbers, and so does a sentence describing the sound.
  • A search embeds your words with the same model and returns the clips whose numbers are closest. No transcript, tags or titles are used.
  • The map squeezes the embeddings to 2-D (UMAP), so neighbours on the map sound alike. Circles are the top matches; click any dot to hear it.
  • “Sounds like this” skips words entirely: it finds the clips whose embedding is closest to the one you picked.
  • ✓ / ✗ come from each dataset's human labels, which the search never sees. Podcast clips have no labels, so they show ?: listen to judge.

Two models, two strengths

  • CLAP (LAION, 2023) was trained on sounds paired with captions, so it can name sound events: cough, sneeze, applause, rain.
  • Gemma (EmbeddingGemma 2, Google) was not trained on captioned sound effects. It is weak at naming events but stronger on how people talk: a news reader, friends joking.
  • From the sister study (audio-vocab): on ESC-50, words find the right sound 90% of the time with CLAP and 25% with Gemma.

What the numbers don't show

  • Emotion is heard but can't be named. Typing “an angry voice” finds almost no angry clips (in CREMA-D the sentence is identical across moods). But “Sounds like this” works above chance: across all angry clips, about 5 of the 10 nearest are also angry (chance 1.7); neutral 3.9, sad 3.2, the rest 2.2–2.6. The model hears the mood more than words can reach it, but only coarsely. The “By example” chips use the most typical clip, so they look better than average.
  • Some words miss. “Clearing throat” and “shouting” reach only 4 of 10: their neighbours are coughs and screams. Here one plain word beat a richer description (0 → 4 of 10).
  • Labels are strict. A ✗ can still sound right (a giggle under “laughter” counts; a laughing crowd in a “clapping” clip doesn't).
  • Podcasts are 20 s clips, the others 2–10 s, so a short cough inside a podcast clip is diluted.

Run it

  • ../audio-vocab/.venv/Scripts/python scripts/serve.py → http://127.0.0.1:8770/ (typed search and audio need the server; chips work offline).
  • Rebuild: scripts/build.py; leave a collection out with --drop podcasts (or emotions / sounds). The layer buttons at the top hide one without rebuilding.
  • Embeddings come from the sister repos (audio-vocab, podcast-vibe-search); nothing is re-trained.
